BICAMERAL CONGRESS
Congress now has two chambers:
- House of Representatives: 100 elected Representatives
- Senate: 100 elected Senators

Citizens elect members of both chambers.
Ordinary citizens do not vote directly on congressional legislation.

BILL PROCESS
1. A Representative or Senator introduces a bill in their chamber.
2. That chamber votes.
3. If it passes, the same bill goes to the other chamber.
4. If both chambers pass it, the bill goes to the President.
5. The President may sign it into Staralia law or veto it.
6. A presidential veto can be overridden only by a two-thirds vote in BOTH chambers.
7. The Supreme Court remains available for constitutional review.

HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES
- 100 numbered seats.
- House elections, candidates, private citizen ballots, and public aggregate tallies.
- Representatives can introduce House bills and vote on House legislation.
- A person cannot simultaneously serve as President, Senator, Representative, and/or Supreme Court Justice where branch separation prohibits it.

SENATE
- 100 numbered seats.
- Senate elections remain in place.
- Senators introduce Senate bills, vote on Senate legislation, confirm Supreme Court nominees, and participate in veto overrides.

PROPOSITIONS
- The referendum system has been completely removed.
- Citizens may vote directly only on numbered Propositions, such as Proposition 6.
- Proposition ballots are private; aggregate Yes/No totals are public to signed-in citizens.
- The two former referendum ballots were preserved and migrated:
  * Proposition 1 — Lunar archive question — existing votes preserved.
  * Proposition 2 — Mars Settlement — existing vote preserved.
- Legacy referendum tables/functions were removed after migration.
